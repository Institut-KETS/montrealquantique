Proposed charter · 12 October 2026

Montréal Quantique governance

Status: proposed rules and initial roles, pending community adoption and institutional confirmation. The proposed CAD 100,000 first allocation is not established as approved, released or currently available.

A community, not a membership body

Montréal Quantique welcomes graduate students, postdoctoral researchers, principal investigators and professors affiliated with a Montréal institute active in quantum science, engineering, technology or education. There are no dues or membership roll. Institutes confirm participation and representatives in writing; an unfilled seat does not limit community participation.

Shared responsibility

Outside the Strategic Advisory Committee, any eligible voter may introduce a motion or call a vote. Ordinary decisions require more yes than no votes; ties fail. Conflicts are disclosed and affected voters recuse. Privacy-safe minutes record motions, votes, owners and financial limits.

Governing bodies

Five connected bodies

Strategic Advisory Committee

The ÉTS Vice-President responsible for research chairs this executive body; the exact English institutional title remains to be confirmed. Olivier Landon-Cardinal is proposed as initial Deputy Chair. Each participating institute has one VP-level vote; if it cannot appoint at that level, its seat remains vacant. The elected student representative has one additional vote.

Jacob Biamonte is proposed as initial Scientific Spokesperson and reports programme and budget results at least quarterly. Only the Spokesperson places ordinary matters on the docket, and only the Chair calls votes. Removal may bypass the docket with 14 days’ notice and requires two-thirds, rounded upward, of all filled, non-recused voting seats.

Speakers and Visitors Selection Committee

Each institute has one vote, plus the elected student vote. The committee selects visitors and programmes talks across the island within a proposed all-in CAD 100,000 envelope, once approved and released, initially intended for one to two years within the permitted spending period.

Selection considers institutional rotation, disciplinary breadth, career stage, conflicts, justified repeat invitations, accessibility, bilingual community reach and travel value. Records distinguish approved, committed and paid costs and report outcomes.

Student Committee

Eligible graduate students elect one representative annually from their body, with one vote on every governing committee. The committee transparently allocates an approved shared programming pool and rotates activities among clubs.

Each participating club should lead one event annually, subject to funds and capacity. CAD 3,000–4,000 per event is an all-in planning norm; eligible food and beverages may be covered. Events require advance ceilings, receipts and outcome reports. Overspends require prior approval.

General Assembly

The annual Assembly is open to all eligible community participants. Meeting-specific affiliation checks establish one-person-one-vote eligibility without creating a membership roll. It governs its own proceedings and voluntary unfunded activity; recommendations concerning institutional duties or funding remain advisory to the competent body.

Communications Committee

Jacob Biamonte and Dmitry Guskov lead the AI-assisted website build. Institutes may appoint one voter each, and the elected student has an additional vote. Publication requires a named factual owner, traceable sources, privacy and consent checks, accessibility, appropriate French and English review, human approval and any applicable ÉTS or MEIE review.

Voting and transparency

Common operating rules

Committees normally receive seven days’ notice. The Assembly, elections, removals and charter amendments receive 14 days; Strategic Advisory meetings always receive 14. Committee quorum is a strict majority of filled, non-recused voting seats, with the body-specific minimums in the complete charter. Abstentions count toward quorum but not votes cast. Electronic ballots preserve notice, eligibility and quorum. Vacancies follow the original appointment or election route.

Financial controls

Programme choice and institutional authority

ÉTS administers the funds. Under the proposed model, Jacob Biamonte is the institutional budget holder and accountable officer, without unilateral programme-allocation power. Committees choose activities within approved envelopes; Jacob verifies or routes spending through ÉTS eligibility, procurement, signing, payroll, reimbursement and audit rules.

An ineligible, unfunded or excessive decision pauses for revision or institutional resolution. No committee, officer or community vote may override ÉTS controls. Adoption of the charter alone releases no funds.

Adoption and amendment

A founding Assembly may endorse the charter. Institutional representation, financial delegation and spending provisions take effect only after confirmation by the competent authorities. Amendments receive 14 days’ notice and Assembly advice, followed by a docketed and Chair-called Strategic Advisory Committee vote supported by two-thirds of filled, non-recused seats and any required institutional confirmation.
